## Question

For each of the blanks ① through ⑥ in the following explanation (which includes a configuration example), select one term from choices A through J below. No term is used in two blanks. The choices include terms that do not fit any of the blanks.

| Step | Attribute compared and the winning condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Highest Weight |
| 2 | Highest LOCAL_PREF |
| 3 | Locally originated route (network/redistribute > aggregate) |
| 4 | Shortest AS_PATH |
| 5 | Lowest ORIGIN (IGP < EGP < Incomplete) |
| 6 | ［①］ |
| 7 | eBGP routes are preferred over iBGP routes |
| 8 | Lowest IGP metric to the next hop |
| 9 | ［②］ |
| 10 | Lowest router ID |
| 11 | ［③］ |
| 12 | ［④］ |

The table above is the BGP best-path selection in Cisco IOS: the candidates for the same prefix are compared in order from the top until they are narrowed down to 1 path. The first 2 steps use values that have meaning only within the local AS, and the value in step 1 is ［⑤］. Step 3 is where a locally originated route is preferred, and a route originated by network/redistribute wins over a route originated by aggregate. From step 4 on, the comparison is of attributes that are carried between autonomous systems. For the origin in step 5, the lower value wins, in the order IGP(i) → EGP(e) → Incomplete(?). By default, the MED in step 6 is compared only between ［⑥］. If the decision is not made by step 8, the older route is kept for the sake of stability, and in the end the tie is broken mechanically in favor of the lower identifier. Notice that when maximum-paths is configured, multiple equal-cost routes are used after step 8; even in this case, only 1 best path is selected.
